If you have only two days

Go in this order. Topics the job description names explicitly carry a badge on the cards above.

WhenDoWhy
Day 1, first block7. Multithreading (sections 1 to 5 first, then 6 to 9), then its quizHighest value: real-time, multi-threaded applications are named in the role, and it is the topic you were least familiar with.
Day 1, second block3. Core Language then its quizRAII, move semantics and smart pointers underpin every other answer.
Day 2, morning5. Templates and 6. Design Patterns, each followed by its quizBoth are named in the job description.
Day 2, afternoon4. STL; then skim 1. Basics and 2. OOP quizzes and read only the sections you missYou probably know much of these. Let the quiz scores decide.
Before the interviewRetake the quizzes you scored under 80% on, and speak the cheat-sheet answers aloud. Then stop studying a couple of hours before.Explaining clearly matters as much as knowing.

Where this comes from

  • Your own notes in this repo: the training notes index (docs/ and examples/) and the learncpp.com recaps.
  • Runnable versions of the lesson snippets (complete programs you can compile): examples/lessons on GitHub.
  • Feature reference: AnthonyCalandra/modern-cpp-features (a cheat sheet of C++11 to C++23 features).
  • Book-style depth: changkun/modern-cpp-tutorial (modern C++ explained chapter by chapter, including its concurrency chapter).

The lessons are original explanations written for this repo; the two projects above were used as references for which features and concepts matter.
