Condition variables

To wait until something happens, a busy loop burns 100% CPU. A condition variable lets the thread sleep until woken, always together with a mutex and the shared state.

The predicate is the truth

The two ideas that make condition variables safe are worth one picture. A thread never trusts a wakeup: it re-checks the condition, under the lock, every time it wakes.

MistakeWhat goes wrongFix
waiting without a predicatea spurious wakeup or a notification that came before the wait is missed or misreadcv.wait(lk, pred)
changing the condition without the mutexthe waiter checks, the notifier changes and notifies, the waiter then sleeps forevermodify the shared state under the same mutex
notify_one when several threads wait for different thingsthe wrong thread wakes and goes back to sleepnotify_all, or separate condition variables

Three rules to follow

  1. Always wait with a predicate. Spurious wakeups happen, and another consumer may take the item first.
  2. Change the state under the mutex, then notify. Otherwise a lost wakeup: the waiter sees false, you notify, it then sleeps forever.
  3. The waiter needs unique_lock: wait unlocks while sleeping.

notify_one() wakes one waiter; notify_all() wakes all (shutdown, or several may proceed).

Debugging and common bugs

Timing bugs vanish under a debugger or a printf ("Heisenbug"). Use tools:

  • ThreadSanitizer: -fsanitize=thread -g finds data races and prints both accesses. Run tests with it.
  • Helgrind / DRD (Valgrind): races and lock-order violations.
  • Thread ids in logs; gdb thread apply all bt on a hang.
SymptomLikely cause
Wrong totals, rare crashesdata race

Vocabulary

  • Thread-safe: usable from several threads without external locking (shared_ptr's reference count, not its pointee).
  • thread_local: one copy per thread.
  • Function-local static init is thread-safe since C++11 (Meyers Singleton).
  • std::call_once(flag, fn): initialize exactly once.

A producer and consumer timeline

The whole pattern is easiest to see on a timeline: the consumer sleeps without using the CPU, the producer pushes and notifies, the consumer wakes, takes the item and sleeps again.

SituationWhat happens
notify arrives before the consumer waitsthe consumer checks the predicate first, sees the item and never sleeps: no lost wakeup
two items pushed, one notifythe consumer pops one, re-checks, finds another and pops that too: a loop around wait handles it
notify with nobody waitingnothing: a condition variable does not remember notifications, the shared state does
shutdownset a stop flag under the lock and notify_all: every waiter wakes, sees the flag and returns

Cheat sheet

Answer aloud, then open.

Why does condition_variable::wait need a predicate / a loop?

Spurious wakeups (a thread may wake without a notification) and because the state may change again between the notification and the thread reacquiring the lock. The predicate re-checks the real condition.

Why must the shared condition be modified under the mutex?

Otherwise the waiter could check the condition (false), then the notifier changes it and notifies, then the waiter goes to sleep: a lost wakeup. The mutex makes check-and-sleep atomic with respect to the update.

How would you find a data race in a large program?

Build and run the tests with ThreadSanitizer (-fsanitize=thread), and Helgrind for lock-order problems; review shared state and make sure every access has a lock or is atomic; reduce sharing by design.

What is a spurious wakeup?

A waiting thread can return from wait without anyone having notified it. That is why the wait is always paired with a predicate that is re-checked after each wakeup.

What does wait do with the mutex?

It must be given a unique_lock. wait unlocks it while the thread sleeps and locks it again before returning, so the predicate is always checked under the lock.
